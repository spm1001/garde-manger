### The sync model — get this right (Sameer flagged it)
Two sync layers; do **not** conflate them:
- **`~/notes` → Syncthing** (Hezza↔Mac, sub-2s on LAN). The garde corpus folds into `notes/raw/` precisely so Mac sees it via *notes'* Syncthing — that IS the "Mac sees the whole archive transparently" mechanism.
- **`~/repos` → git** (Hezza canonical, Mac auto-pulls). `.git` is deliberately **not** Syncthing'd (corruption risk). garde-manger lives in `~/repos`, so it syncs by git, not Syncthing.
We did **not** plan to Syncthing `~/repos`. The archive's transparency comes from landing in notes, not from where the repo lives.

## For Claudes to come

A tool's apparatus can be over-built for its actual job, and only measurement reveals it. garde was a 692 MB SQLite+FTS5 system with an extraction pipeline, a backfill cron, and an unrealised Dolt/multi-machine roadmap — but the *job* was only ever "clean finished sessions and make them findable." Measuring the payload collapsed the edifice: deglacer compresses raw CC JSONL ~74× (4.9 GB → 67 MB), the real signal is ~1,300 sessions / ~41 MB, and 41 MB rides an existing Syncthing'd notes repo for free — so the cross-machine "problem" that justified Dolt simply dissolved. The transferable lesson: before building sync/scale/index machinery for a data tool, measure the actual payload and the actual usage; the apparatus may be retireable. A Claude with grep out-searches BM25 at small scale because it judges relevance rather than just ranking it, and "fold the small thing into a system that already exists" beats "build bespoke infrastructure." garde's last act is one final parse — exporting its sole-surviving sessions into notes — then it goes dark, its idea living on in the digester it helped specify.
