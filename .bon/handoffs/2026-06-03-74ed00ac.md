## For Claudes to come

When decommissioning a tool by folding it into an existing system, inventory the destination's assets before specifying new format or tooling — the destination may have a head start the plan didn't credit. Here, briefs written to treat the archive-format step as greenfield were disproved by a five-minute look at the target directory: notes' earlier work already had the landing dir, a working deglacer-based converter, and a frontmatter convention, so the step collapsed from "invent" to "reconcile, and note the deltas." Corollary, equally load-bearing: a "one last parse" from a dying tool is rarely monolithic. Split the corpus by *what still needs the tool* — here, ~1,300 disk-resident sessions can be parsed straight from their source files (the dying tool never enters the loop), and only ~106 genuinely-orphaned sessions (source deleted, surviving only in the tool's DB) require it. Finding that split early shrinks the irreversible, tool-dependent work to its true core and lets the rest proceed without risk to the ark.
